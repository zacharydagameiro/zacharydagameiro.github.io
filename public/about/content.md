## About me
I'm a Computing Science and Biochemistry student at SFU, focused on systems and applied ML. I like building things end-to-end, from embedded hardware to pipelines and full-stack apps, and I'm always up for a tough problem. When I'm not coding, I'm usually outdoors or traveling.

## Areas
- **Frontend:** I enjoy building frontend interfaces when there is real product depth to them, especially dashboards, data-heavy screens, forms, and UI with a lot of moving parts. I spend a lot of time thinking about layout, interaction details, and how to make a product usable without making it feel cluttered.
- **Backend:** I like backend work around APIs, auth, validation, permissions, data models, and writing the logic that actually makes an app work. A lot of the work I enjoy most is taking something messy in the real world and turning it into a backend that is structured and predictable.
- **Mobile:** I enjoy building mobile apps and spending time on the details that make them feel good to use, like navigation, gestures, haptics, and screen-to-screen transitions. I like mobile when it feels like its own product, not just a smaller web app.
- **Biotech and computational biology:** I’m in school for microbiology and biochemistry as well, so I’m naturally interested in software that overlaps with biology. That includes bioinformatics, biological data, scientific tools, and computational problems related to proteins, cells, and molecular systems.

## Curiosity areas
- **AI for biology:** I follow work like **AlphaFold**, protein structure prediction, and the broader use of machine learning for biological discovery.
- **Quantum computing for biology:** I'm curious about how quantum methods might eventually matter for problems like **protein folding** and molecular simulation.
- **Drug discovery and simulation:** I’m interested in biological and chemical simulation for **drug R&D**, including drug interaction modeling and structure-based discovery.
- **Personalized medicine:** I’m especially interested in the long-term idea of designing or matching drugs using an individual's sequenced genome.
